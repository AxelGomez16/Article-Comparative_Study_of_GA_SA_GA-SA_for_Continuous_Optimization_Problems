  \subsection*{Rastrigin Function Problem}
    The function of Rastrigin is a non-convex, non-linear and multimodal function, being n-dimensional 
    and denotated at the equation \ref{eq:Rastrigin}.
  
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Rastrigin}
      f(\mathbf{x}) = An + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left[ x_i^2 - A\cos(2\pi x_i) \right]
    \end{equation}

    Where: 
    \begin{itemize} 
      \item $f(\mathbf{x})$ is the objective function to minimize 
      \item $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, x_2, x_3, \ldots, x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is a vector of variables 
      \item $A$ is a constant, usually $A = 10$, denoting the amplitude of oscillation 
      \item $n$ is the dimension of the vector $\mathbf{x}$ 
      \item $ -5.12 \leq x_i \leq 5.12$ is the commonly used search space. 
      \item The global minimum is at $\mathbf{x} = (0, 0, 0, \ldots, 0)$ with a value of $f(\mathbf{x}) = 0$ 
    \end{itemize}

    The Rastrigin function is frequently used to evaluate the performance of optimization
    algorithms due to its complexity. The quadratic term $x_i^2$ creates a global minimum,
    while the term $-A\cos(2\pi x_i)$ introduces periodic oscillations with amplitude $A$.
    When combined with the quadratic term, these produce approximately $10^n$ local minima,
    determined by the oscillation frequency of the $\cos(2\pi x_i)$ term and the 
    search space \cite{omeradzic2022progress}.

    Consequently, the Rastrigin function poses a significant challenge in determining 
    whether an algorithm can identify local minima without becoming trapped in them, 
    thereby enabling it to locate the global minimum \cite{benhamou2019bcma}.

\section*{Metaheuristic Algorithms}

  The main difference between Classic Algorithms (CA) and Metaheuristic Algorithms (MA) 
  can be pointed out in quality solution, due to the CA can find an exact and optimal solution, 
  meanwhile the MA can find an approximate and acceptable solution \cite{almufti2017using}.
  
  The results of the set of problems presented in the previous section are following these
  four criteria:

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item It does not guarantee the optimal solution, however it would find an acceptable
      solution and nearly optimal solution in a reasonable time.
    \item An incorrect architecture of the MA can lead to premature convergence, which 
      means an incorrect solution and wasted computational time.
    \item The performacne of any MA is excessive sensible to the configuration of it.
    \item The computational time of any MA would be higly due to the number of iterations.  
  \end{enumerate}

  To solve the problems displayed in this article is estrictly necessary to test out diffetent MA,
  in consecuence of the theorem of \textit{``No Free Lunch''} which means that there is no better
  MA than another for a particular problem, thus various MA must be tested to find the best MA for 
  the problem \cite{almufti2022lion}.

  The four categories of MA are:

  \begin{itemize}
    \item Evolutionary Algorithms (EA): The theory of evolution by Darwin mentions that the 
      most fit organisms can survive and reproduce, meanwhile the less fit 
      organisms would extinct \cite{garg2010evolutionary}.
    
    \item Physical Processes (PP): The laws of physics and natural phenomena can be used as
      starting point to solve problems, where the gravity, temperature, radiation, etc. can be 
      used as inspiration to solve problems \cite{agrawal2021metaheuristic}.

    \item Swarm Intelligence (SI): It based by the nature and the behavior of the animals, 
      where it tries to mimic the the behaviour of them to achive the supervivence of the scpecies,
      by way of example, ants, wolfs, bees, birds, etc \cite{almufti2022lion}.

    \item Human Behavior (HB): The way of behave of humans across the society works as inspiration to 
      develop new MA able to solve complex problems \cite{agrawal2021metaheuristic}
        
  \end{itemize}


  In this section, the most popular and studied MA are being presented, which are GA,
